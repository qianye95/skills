# Corpus-derived epic-xianxia profile

## Narrative lens

- Default to a close third-person viewpoint during action, then widen briefly to terrain, history, crowds, or celestial scale when the widening changes the reader's understanding.
- Move from the visible to the hidden: establish an intelligible surface, introduce one anomaly, show its consequence, then disclose a deeper layer.
- State important reversals clearly. Do not bury the scene-changing fact inside decorative description.
- Use authoritative exposition sparingly and connect it to an active mystery, danger, decision, or comparison.

## Rhythm

The source corpus has a median sentence length of 29 Han characters, while 20.9% of sentences are 10 characters or shorter and 25.0% are 40 characters or longer. Reproduce the contrast, not a fixed template.

- Use medium-to-long sentences for space, causal chains, historical depth, and layered perception.
- Use short sentences for recognition, reversal, impact, refusal, or a newly named threat.
- Avoid three consecutive paragraphs with the same sentence-length pattern.
- Keep paragraphs compact: one primary beat per paragraph.
- Let punctuation follow meaning. Do not inflate intensity by adding exclamation marks mechanically.

## Scale ladder

Build magnitude through ordered comparison:

1. a bodily sensation, gesture, sound, or object;
2. a room, formation, battlefield, city, or landscape;
3. a celestial, historical, civilizational, or temporal implication.

Use no more scale levels than the scene earns. Every level must add information, not merely synonyms for “large” or “terrible.”

## Progressive revelation

- Begin with a stable expectation.
- Introduce a contradiction that can be perceived but not yet explained.
- Let characters form a plausible partial interpretation.
- Break that interpretation with a concrete new fact.
- Name or frame the deeper problem near the end of the sequence.

Do not repeat “everyone was shocked” after every reveal. Reactions should differentiate knowledge, allegiance, fear, ambition, or tactical response.

## Combat feedback loop

Organize major exchanges as:

1. threat signal;
2. decision or countermeasure;
3. technique expressed through motion and constraint;
4. material consequence;
5. information update about strength, cost, or hidden capability;
6. changed tactic or reversal.

Make combat spatially legible. Track who can see, reach, escape, interrupt, or protect what. Use named techniques only when their function is understandable from the scene.

## Power and status

- Demonstrate power through consequences before explaining rank.
- Use comparisons to established benchmarks, but do not turn every encounter into a hierarchy lecture.
- Let status change behavior: silence, retreat, bargaining, formation changes, broken assumptions, or opportunistic alliances.
- Preserve uncertainty above the current ceiling so later escalation remains possible.

## Dialogue

Dialogue paragraphs account for roughly one third of the source corpus, with a higher proportion early and a lower proportion late.

- Use dialogue to challenge knowledge, stake a position, conceal intent, or force a choice.
- Keep battle dialogue concise.
- Distinguish speakers through objectives and information, not repeated verbal tics.
- As scale widens, reduce chatter and increase consequential decisions and narration.

## Chapter architecture

- Open with movement, a concrete state, or an unresolved consequence.
- Place a meaningful update every few paragraphs: new fact, cost, choice, comparison, or reversal.
- Deliver at least one earned payoff per chapter.
- End by changing the reader's question. A hook may reveal a larger threat, expose a hidden identity, impose a cost, or force a decision.
- Prefer compact event-bearing titles, typically four to eight Han characters.

## Anti-caricature rules

- Do not stack “ancient,” “terrifying,” “mysterious,” “boundless,” and similar scale adjectives as a substitute for evidence.
- Do not overuse suddenness markers, collective shock, pupil contraction, silence, or boiling crowds.
- Do not alternate long exposition and one-line exclamations on a mechanical schedule.
- Do not copy source-specific cosmic imagery, artifacts, cultivation systems, character relationships, or event sequences.
- Do not write a renamed version of an existing chapter.
